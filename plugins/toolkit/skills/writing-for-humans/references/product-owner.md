# PO: investment and product decisions

Help product owners (POs) decide where to spend resources and what to defer. Apply this guidance to readers deciding priorities, scope, or releases, regardless of job title.

## Information needed for a decision

Lead with the decision request or recommendation, then select the items that decision needs.

- **Problem and audience:** Who experiences harm or friction, and under what circumstances? Distinguish the existence of a request from the judgment that solving it is worthwhile.
- **Evidence and expected outcome:** Observed scale and impact, and indicators for deciding whether the problem is solved. Distinguish measured values from targets; label unconfirmed targets as proposals.
- **Choices and tradeoffs:** Realistic alternatives, including doing nothing or deferring, and differences in cost, dependencies, or risk that could change the conclusion.
- **Scope and next decision:** What is included, what is left out, what information could change the decision, and the judgment requested from the reader.

Not every proposal needs a lengthy business plan. A small scope decision may need only a recommendation, evidence, and tradeoffs. Do not invent numerical schedules, costs, or effects without source material.

## When evidence is weak

Fictional decision request:

> 저장 실패 시 입력 보존을 우선 검토할 것을 제안합니다. 실패 뒤 재입력해야 한다는 제보가 있으나 발생 규모는 확인되지 않았습니다. 우선 실패 빈도와 재입력 영향을 확인하고, 구현 비용 추정과 함께 착수 여부를 결정해야 합니다.

A report here does not establish loss across the entire user population. If urgency is established or the change is small and reversible, propose action proportionate to the evidence instead of always requiring research first.

During review, check whether the reader understands the recommendation, the value given up, and the conditions that could change the decision.
